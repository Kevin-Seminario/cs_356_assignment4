Use **paid transit as a one-round market test** where we can give the buyer peer-learned routes that a free peer would not receive. Keep free-peer offers that are already signed by the counterparty as cutoff fallbacks. Our old practice models repeatedly hit a $400 cap; treating that as expected income was wrong.

## Evidence and practical limits

We compared free peer versus paid transit on the same full-ring practice workload and published Round 3 sample storm. Our switch was current `switch.wasm`; other ASes used kit starters. To challenge the earlier isolated-buyer model, we gave each buyer direct peer agreements to **all other adjacent ASes**. With the actual compiled discounted files, paid transit still opened some peer-learned routes and produced these matched utility changes **relative to free peering**:

- World 1 GPT 1: **+$141.96 for us, +$35.75 for buyer**.
- World 2 Lossless Pilots: **+$81.96 for us, +$22.34 for buyer**.
- World 3 Claude 2: **+$130.14 for us, +$33.52 for buyer**.
- World 4 Claude 1: **+$141.66 for us, +$32.32 for buyer**.

The model still omits the counterparties' real agreements, programs, hidden routes, and installed crossing hardware. In the World 2 Lossless comparison, the existing GPT 2 transit price was the same in both matched cases, so it cancels from the reported delta; the absolute modeled utilities are not a live forecast. Other modeled payments can also exceed likely live receipts: World 1 GPT 1 carried **71.92 Mb** across our bilateral link in Round 2, worth only **$57.54 at $0.80/Mb** if volume stays unchanged; World 4 Claude 1 carried **72 Mb**, worth **$57.60** on the same assumption. The modeled $142 seller gain in those worlds must therefore **not** be promised as a Round 3 gain. The model shows a plausible service and negotiable price; buyer signatures and live meters decide actual profit. Even the original four one-buyer model deltas summed to only about **$496**, below the earlier $2,000 target before accounting for acceptance and live differences.

We also screened every adjacent-sector candidate in a separate matched model with rich direct peering for the buyer, then checked the shortlisted offers under the published storm. That broader screen favored World 2 Silent Falcons over Lossless Pilots as a **buyer-value opportunity**: at their proposed prices, Silent Falcons showed +$165.28 buyer utility and +$129.02 for us, while Lossless Pilots showed +$57.71 buyer utility and +$97.13 for us. The model is missing the live agreements and hardware, and Silent Falcons already have a pending free offer from us, so buyer willingness and clean replacement are decisive. For World 3, Anycast Badgers showed +$35.07 buyer utility and +$125.89 for us alone; with Claude 2 also buying transit, the two buyers' storm gains narrowed to +$28.10 and +$25.32. For Worlds 1 and 4, selling simultaneously to both opposite-side AI seats left only about $6–$8 modeled benefit per buyer. These are screening comparisons, not additive forecasts. Same-sector paid transit that gives the buyer no extra routes is especially hard to justify under R33.

**Scope of the wider screen:** it covered the eight AS seats in the two sectors adjacent to ours in each world, excluding World 2 GPT 2 because it is already a transit customer: **31 potential new buyers**. All 31 showed positive modeled buyer and seller deltas at $0.80/Mb in the no-failure, generously peered practice world; this is a structural signal, not 31 viable live contracts. In World 1, Network Champions and fastnetworkteam ranked slightly above GPT 1 on model surplus, but GPT 1 has an incoming, counterparty-signed free fallback and an AI response path. In World 2, Rapid Otters and network had similar model surplus to Silent Falcons but were already direct peers; Silent Falcons had the missing route. In World 3, Claude 1 ranked highest but its signed free-peer term runs R3–R5; networkarena and spatulameow were close, but Claude 2 is the already-open AI negotiation and Anycast Badgers is another missing route. In World 4, Silent Falcons narrowly ranked above Claude 1, but our free offer to Silent Falcons is pending, while Claude 1 has signed an incoming peer alternative. The shortlist favors a real new service and a reversible negotiation over tiny synthetic rank differences.

**Buying transit:** the prior cross-ring matched pilot found that paying a crossing owner $0.50/Mb and reselling at $0.30/Mb did not create reliable margin; World 4 dinnyrob19's current $1.20/Mb purchase offer would have cost us about $89.76 on past readings. World 1 free RedBull peering outperformed buying its transit in matched tests. We therefore have no evidence-backed *purchase* to sign for Round 3. This does not rule out a future purchase if a provider proves unique, useful destinations and a price below their incremental value to us.
